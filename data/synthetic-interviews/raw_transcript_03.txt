INTERVIEWER: How do you usually handle a call where a customer reports 'cloudy' or 'milky' water and is worried about their health?

AGENT: Alright, yeah, so cloudy or milky water calls... yeah, I get those a lot. First thing, I gotta figure out *exactly* what they're seeing, right? Is it actually cloudy, like, opaque-ish, or is it more of a milky, white-ish thing that *sort of* clears from the bottom up if you let it sit in a glass for a few minutes? Because that's a big difference.
If it's truly cloudy, I'm thinking turbidity right off the bat. You know, suspended solids. Ask 'em if they've noticed any construction nearby, or a fire hydrant being flushed. Main break, even. That can stir things up in the mains. Gotta let 'em know it's probably just sediment and usually not a health risk... *usually*. But, still, I'll log it as a water quality concern, flag the address for possible flushing, and advise them to run the cold water tap outside for, like, fifteen minutes. See if that clears it. If it's still messed up after that, then yeah, we'll probably dispatch a tech to check the system pressure and maybe grab a sample for testing, just to be sure.
Now, if it's more milky, like, white-ish... that's almost always just air. Happens, especially during colder months when the water's colder and holds more dissolved air. And it doesn’t take much to look really terrible! Ask 'em to fill a clear glass, let it sit. If the cloudiness clears from the bottom up in a few minutes, tell 'em it's just air, perfectly harmless. Usually, that's all it is. But, still, I document it, just in case.
Regardless, I always try to be sympathetic, you know? “I understand your concern, ma'am/sir, water quality *is* important.” Even though I’m thinking in my head, like, “Please, it’s just air, you’re wasting my time,” I still gotta be professional, right? Especially if they’re worried about their health, I'll say, "If you’re *genuinely* concerned, especially if you're immunocompromised or have underlying health conditions, you should consult with your doctor before drinking it." You know, cover my butt. Always gotta CYA in this job, you know? And then, you know, reassure them that we're taking it seriously, even if it's just air. Because to *them*, it's their drinking water, and they're freaked out. So, yeah, that's pretty much it, um, unless it’s something really weird, like, I had this one call where…nevermind. You get the idea. Cover all the bases, be polite, document *everything*. It’s a long night.


INTERVIEWER: How do you explain a sudden spike in a water bill to someone who is adamant that their usage habits haven't changed at all?

AGENT: Alright, yeah, so, sudden spike in usage... I've heard that one a few times. Okay, so first, I try to stay calm. I know, I know, everyone thinks they're using the exact same amount of water they always do, but water doesn't just magically, like, appear on the bill. There's a reason.
Um, the first thing I'll do is ask them about any *actual* changes, like, even small ones. "Did you have guests? Fill a pool? Water the lawn more? Even a small drip in the irrigation system can add up, right?" Sometimes people just forget, or they, like, don't think it's a big deal.
If they're still saying no way, no how, then I'll start going technical. "Okay, so, have you checked all your fixtures for leaks? Even the ones you don't see? Under sinks, behind toilets? I mean, a running toilet, even if it's a slow one, that can *really* run up your meter, especially with the new electronic meters."
Then, I'll explain how to check their meter themselves. “Go out to your meter. Make sure no water is being used in the house and see if the low flow indicator, the little red or black star, is spinning. If it is, water is flowing somewhere.” I might tell them, um, you know, depending on the model, “look for a number sequence. Read it. Then wait an hour without using any water and check it again. If that number increased, you have a leak”.
After that, I might ask about their sprinkler system, if they have one. Those things can be sneaky. A broken head, a clogged nozzle, a leak in the line… It all adds up. And sometimes, those leaks are underground and you don't even see them.
Okay, so, if all that's clear, and they're *still* adamant, then I have to start considering less likely, but still possible, issues. Uh, "Do you have a backflow preventer on your irrigation system? Those can fail and cause water to flow backwards into the system, although it is rare." And finally, I have to mention the possibility of a reading error or, you know, tampering with the meter. Although, usually, tampering is pretty obvious.
And, I mean, I’ll, I'll say, "Look, I know it's frustrating, but we have to rule everything out. I can schedule a meter re-read, and we can send a technician out to check for leaks on the service line between the main and your meter. There is a cost, but if it is our problem we will cover it. But it will take a few days because we have a few other calls ahead of you". Usually that makes them happy, because at least we're doing *something*. But honestly, nine times out of ten, it's a hidden leak they didn't know about. It’s always a leak.
